Short answer: white-label email development means a specialist team builds HTML emails, templates and Klaviyo flows for your agency's clients, and you deliver the work under your own brand. The client never deals with the partner. It lets agencies take on more email work, offer email as a new service and keep their margins, without hiring and training email developers.

Many web, design and marketing agencies get asked for email work sooner or later. A client wants a new newsletter template, a Klaviyo welcome flow or a fix for emails that break in Outlook. Saying no sends that client to another agency. Saying yes without the right skills can lead to broken emails and unhappy clients. A white-label partner sits in the middle: you keep the client relationship, and the partner does the specialist build quietly in the background.

Why Agencies Use a White-Label Email Partner

  • Email is specialist work. Coding emails that work in Outlook, Gmail and dark mode is very different from building websites. Email still relies on tables, inline styles and fallbacks for old rendering engines. A strong web developer can lose days learning these rules.
  • Demand is uneven. Some months you have ten email projects, some months none. A partner scales with you, so you are not paying a full-time salary during quiet months.
  • Faster delivery. A specialist team codes and tests an email in 24 to 48 hours, because they have done the same work many times and have a tested base of code to start from.
  • New revenue. You can sell email templates, Klaviyo flows and campaign builds without building a team first. Email becomes a line on your service menu instead of a request you turn down.
  • Less risk. A broken email goes to thousands of people at once. Handing the build to a team that tests every send lowers the chance of a public mistake with your client's brand.

How the Process Works

  1. You sell the project and agree the brief with your client.
  2. You send the design (Figma, PSD or XD) or brief to the partner, with the platform and deadline.
  3. The partner builds and tests the email in 50+ clients and returns files or uploads to the client's platform under your account.
  4. You review, present it to your client as your own work and handle feedback.
  5. Revisions go back to the partner and return quickly.

The key point is that you stay the single point of contact. The partner never emails your client, never joins their calls and never appears in shared folders. If the partner needs platform access, it is best to give it through a user seat you control, so you can remove it at any time.

What a good brief includes

Most delays come from missing information, not slow coding. A short brief saves a round of questions:

  • The design file with all states: desktop, mobile and any hover or dark mode notes.
  • The email platform (Klaviyo, Mailchimp, HubSpot, Salesforce Marketing Cloud or a plain HTML file).
  • Which sections must be editable by the client's team.
  • Fonts, brand colours, link URLs and any tracking parameters.
  • The deadline, and whether the files should be uploaded or just delivered.

Setting your own margin

Your price to the client should cover the partner's fee, your project management time, client meetings and revisions you handle yourself. Many agencies price email work the same way they price design work: by the value to the client and the time spent on communication, not only by the partner's cost. Keep a simple sheet of partner prices per deliverable so your team can quote quickly and consistently.

How to Choose a White-Label Email Partner

  1. No branding: the partner's name should never appear in code, files or emails. Check HTML comments, image file names and upload folders.
  2. NDA: they should sign one on request and never contact your clients.
  3. Testing: ask which clients they test in; Outlook and dark mode should be included. Our testing checklist shows what good QA covers.
  4. Platform experience: Klaviyo, Mailchimp, HubSpot and Salesforce Marketing Cloud at minimum.
  5. Turnaround and communication: clear deadlines and fast replies in your time zone.
  6. A test project: start with one email before committing to volume.

Red flags to watch for

  • A partner who asks to speak to your client directly "to save time".
  • Code full of builder output or classes named after another company.
  • No clear answer about which Outlook versions they test.
  • Vague revision terms, or extra charges for small text changes.
  • No written turnaround time.

A quick code check

When the first template arrives, open the HTML and look at a few basics. A clean file should use tables for layout, have Outlook conditional comments where needed, and carry no stray branding. For example, a well-built file often includes a block like this for classic Outlook:

<!--[if mso]><table role="presentation" width="600"><tr><td><![endif]-->

You do not need to read every line. You just want to see signs that the partner understands email rendering, not only web layout.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is white-label email development?

It is email development done by a specialist partner and delivered under your agency's brand, so your client sees your agency as the provider.

Will my clients know I use a partner?

Not if the partner is truly white-label: no branding in files or code, no contact with your clients, and an NDA if you need one.

How fast can a white-label partner deliver?

A single email template is usually coded and tested in 24 to 48 hours once the design is ready. Larger projects follow an agreed timeline.

Do I need to give the partner access to my client's email platform?

Only if you want them to upload templates or build flows. Give access through a user seat you control, and remove it when the project ends. Otherwise, the partner can deliver HTML files for your team to upload.

Which email platforms can a white-label partner work with?

Most specialist partners work with Klaviyo, Mailchimp, HubSpot and Salesforce Marketing Cloud, and can also deliver plain HTML for other platforms. Always confirm before you quote your client.
