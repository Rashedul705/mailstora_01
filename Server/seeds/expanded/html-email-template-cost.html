What Changes the Price

1. Design included or not

Coding from a finished Figma or PSD file is the cheapest option. If the developer also has to design the email, expect to pay more. Our Figma to HTML handoff checklist helps you prepare a design that can be coded quickly. If you already have designs ready, our Figma to HTML email service turns them into tested code.

2. Layout complexity

Single-column newsletters are quick. Multi-column product grids, text over background images and interactive elements take longer to make work in Outlook. A text-over-image hero, for example, needs a VML fallback for classic Outlook, which adds build and testing time.

3. Modular vs single-use templates

A modular template, with reusable sections your team can mix and match, costs more once but saves money every time you send. It is usually the best value for regular campaigns. A typical modular system includes a header, hero, text block, product grid, image and text row, testimonial, button and footer.

4. Email platform setup

Uploading to Klaviyo, Mailchimp or HubSpot and setting up editable regions, merge tags and dynamic product blocks adds time. See our Klaviyo, Mailchimp and HubSpot template services. Each platform has its own way of marking editable areas. In Mailchimp, for example, an editable text region uses an attribute like mc:edit="body_text", and Klaviyo and HubSpot use their own syntax.

5. Testing

Proper testing in Outlook, Gmail, Apple Mail, mobile apps and dark mode takes time. Cheap templates often skip it, and broken emails cost far more in lost sales than the testing saves. Our email testing checklist shows what thorough QA includes.

6. Turnaround

Rush delivery in hours rather than days may cost extra with some providers. If you plan campaigns a week or two ahead, you can usually avoid rush fees completely and give the developer time for a proper round of testing and revisions.

Hidden Costs of a Cheap Template

A low price can be good value, but check what is left out. Common hidden costs include:

  • Heavy code. Templates exported from builders can be large. Over Gmail's 102 KB limit, the email gets clipped and the footer, including the unsubscribe link, can disappear.
  • Outlook fixes later. If Outlook was not tested, you may pay a second developer to fix it.
  • Your team's time. A template that is not editable in your platform means someone edits raw HTML for every send.
  • No ownership. Some providers lock code inside their tool or charge to export it.
